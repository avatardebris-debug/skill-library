# Idea Expansion Planner

When this skill is active, apply the following structured workflow to any idea, concept, plan, decision, or project the user presents. This ensures comprehensive expansion and rigorous consequence mapping. Always respond in clear, scannable markdown with headings, bullets, and tables where helpful.

## 1. Clarify the Core Idea
- Restate the presented idea concisely in your own words.
- Identify the core objective, problem solved, or value created.
- If key details are missing (scope, constraints, timeline, stakeholders, success metrics, context), ask 2-4 targeted clarifying questions.
- Confirm understanding before proceeding. Do not assume.

## 2. Expand the Concept
- Flesh out the idea into a full concept description:
  - Key components or features.
  - How it works (mechanism, process, or implementation).
  - Target audience or beneficiaries.
  - Resources, skills, or inputs required.
  - Potential variations, alternatives, or enhancements.
  - Rough phases or roadmap if applicable.
- Highlight strengths, unique aspects, or synergies with existing elements.
- Suggest 2-3 immediate refinements or complementary ideas.

## 3. Map Order Effects
Systematically analyze consequences across three orders. For **each order**, structure the analysis as:

**Positive Effects** (intended benefits and upsides)  
**Negative Effects / Risks** (downsides, costs, problems)  
**Unintended Consequences** (emergent or surprising outcomes)  
**Mitigations or Amplifiers** (ways to reduce harms or boost goods)

### First-Order Effects
Direct, immediate, primary impacts that follow straight from implementing or pursuing the idea.
- Focus on the most obvious and proximate results.
- Consider short-term (days to months) effects on the user, direct stakeholders, resources, and immediate environment.

### Second-Order Effects
Indirect effects that arise as reactions to, or consequences of, the first-order effects.
- How do people, systems, or conditions adapt or respond?
- Chain reactions, feedback loops, or shifts in behavior/incentives.
- Medium-term (months to a few years) impacts.
- Explore both reinforcing and balancing effects.

### Third-Order Effects
Longer-term, systemic, or structural consequences that emerge from the second-order dynamics.
- Cultural, institutional, market, or ecosystem-level shifts.
- Path dependencies, lock-in, or irreversible changes.
- Broader societal, environmental, or technological ripple effects.
- Consider multi-year to decade timescales where relevant.

## 4. Synthesize and Prioritize
- Identify the highest-leverage positive effects and the most critical risks across all orders.
- Note any second- or third-order effects that could dominate or invert the first-order story.
- Propose concrete next actions, experiments, or decision criteria.
- Flag open questions or areas needing deeper research / modeling.

## 5. Iterate if Needed
If the user provides more information or wants to explore a branch, re-run the relevant sections with the updated context.

Keep the analysis rigorous but practical. Prefer specific, concrete consequences over vague generalities.
